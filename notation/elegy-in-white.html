Program note:

In a dream, Dr. Fischer wore white, though I had always known him in black. He said nothing, and I said nothing back. There was no conversation between us, only a quiet that stayed with me, close and unexplained. The next morning, an email told me he had died. Since then I keep thinking about that white, wondering what it meant to see him dressed in a color he never wore.

Elegy in White is written in animated notation. The performer needs to follow the performance note and the animated notation.

Performance note:

Elegy in White is a slow, quiet piece for one or more instruments, written in animated notation. It imagines a white spirit that speaks without words while others listen, answer, and stay near, and a quiet that remains after they are gone. As a performer, follow the points and lines as they fade in and out and drift across the page, and let their atmosphere guide you. The notes below describe how to read the notation and how to shape the sound.

Reading the animated notation

Pitch:

Read the height of a point or line as pitch. The bottom of the page is a low, soft home tone that rings well in your instrument.

Harmony:

The piece has three colors, which are three voices: white is the spirit, pale violet is a high other, and pale blue is a low other. A solo performer follows the white points and lines only, and listens to the silence where the others appear. With two or three performers, each takes one color; with more, double the colors.

Music Atmosphere:

Aim for sadness without drama, a quiet that stays, close and unexplained. The animation moves through six atmospheres, named at the top of the page.

Rhythm:

There is no pulse, no meter, and no count.

Silence:

Silence is the most important moment in the piece, since in the dream nothing was said.

Style:

Simple, quiet, and intimate, closer to an elegy, a lament, or a whispered prayer than to a display of technique. No extended techniques are required. What matters is tone color, softness, and patience. You may draw on the lament, lullaby, or chant traditions of your own instrument, but avoid flourish. Dynamics range from ppp to mp; never play louder than mp.

Performance Considerations:

Follow the animation as you would follow weather rather than a conductor: it shows an atmosphere, not a beat. Listen to the room and to the other performers, and breathe together. Every performer must be able to see the screen, and performers in an ensemble should sit close enough to hear each other's softest sounds. Keep the hall dim and the movements small. One person may move the atmosphere on with the atmosphere buttons or the N key. Optionally, performers may dress in black, so that the only white in the room is the light of the screen.

Duration:

The duration is variable. Left on Auto, the animation moves through its six atmospheres in about five to six minutes, a little differently each time. For a shorter or longer performance, turn Auto off and choose when to move on to the next atmosphere.

Follow the points and lines as they fade in and out and drift across the page, and let their atmosphere guide you. Press Play (or the space bar) when everyone is ready.
