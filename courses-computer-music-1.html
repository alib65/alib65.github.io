Computer Music I

MUCP 4341-001 • Fall 2026

Computer Music I provides an introductory survey of digital sound production, synthesis, and composition. Students explore the fundamental concepts and practical applications of computer music across multiple platforms and paradigms. This course serves as a comprehensive introduction to the landscape of computer music technologies, covering audio fundamentals, digital signal processing concepts, and hands-on experience with industry-standard and open-source music software.

Course Content

This course introduces the core concepts and tools of computer music through both theoretical understanding and practical application. Topics include:

  • Audio Fundamentals: Sampling, bit depth, sample rate, signal flow, and audio analysis
  • Digital Audio Workstations (DAWs): Multitrack recording, MIDI sequencing, audio editing, mixing, and mastering using industry-standard software
  • Sound Synthesis: Subtractive, additive, FM, and granular synthesis techniques; oscillators, envelopes, and filters
  • Introduction to Real-Time Music Programming: SuperCollider and Pure Data/Max MSP for algorithmic composition, live coding, and interactive systems
  • Hardware Integration: MIDI controllers, audio interfaces, analog synthesizers, and hardware-software integration
  • Music Software Ecosystems: Exploring open-source and commercial environments for sound design and composition

Course Purpose & Learning Objectives

This course develops foundational competencies in computer music production and sound design. Upon completion, students will be able to:

  • Demonstrate understanding of audio fundamentals and digital signal processing concepts
  • Operate digital audio workstations and multitrack recording environments effectively
  • Design and create sounds using multiple synthesis methods and sound design techniques
  • Program music using visual and text-based programming environments (Pure Data, Max MSP, SuperCollider)
  • Compose and produce original music and audio projects incorporating various computer music techniques
  • Integrate hardware and software tools for music production and performance
  • Critically evaluate different computer music tools and approaches for creative applications

Final grades reflect student engagement, technical competency, and creative achievement. Evaluation is based on the following components:

  • Homework Assignments & Technical Exercises (30%): Weekly assignments demonstrating skill development
  • Class Participation & Engagement (20%): Attendance, active participation, peer collaboration, presentations

Grading Scale: A: 90-100% | B: 80-89% | C: 70-79% | D: 60-69% | F: Below 60%

Final Project Guidelines

Students will create an original computer music composition or interactive sound design project incorporating techniques learned throughout the course. Projects should demonstrate mastery of at least two different technologies/approaches (e.g., DAW + synthesis, Pure Data + hardware, SuperCollider composition, etc.). Projects must be accompanied by documentation including:

  • A written description of the project concept and artistic intent (250–500 words)
  • Technical documentation explaining software/hardware used and signal flow
  • Audio files or video documentation of the completed work
  • Code or patch files (where applicable) for reproduction and examination
