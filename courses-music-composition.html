Music Composition II

MUCP 2201-001 • Fall 2026

Music Composition II is a project-based studio course in which each student composes one substantial original work over the full semester, taking it from an initial idea to a finished, presentation-ready version. In the first class meeting, each student selects a project in one of three tracks: (1) concert music for acoustic instruments or voices; (2) electronic and electroacoustic music, including fixed media, live electronics, and multichannel work; or (3) music for media, including animation, film, and video games. Hybrid projects that combine tracks, such as an ensemble with fixed media or an animated short scored for live instruments, are encouraged. Each weekly session combines listening and score study with critique of the student's current draft, so that every new version of the piece responds to documented feedback on the last.

Course Content

Topics are introduced as each project requires them. Each student works in depth within their own track rather than covering every topic. Topics include:

  • Concept and Planning: Artistic intent, project brief, model works and precedents, form plan, and production timeline
  • Materials and Development: Motive, harmony, modality and microtonality, rhythm, texture, and timbre; growing a small idea into a large form
  • Form and Time: Sectional and through-composed forms, pacing, climax, and proportion; timing, cue lists, and hit points in media projects
  • Orchestration and Instrumentation: Ranges, idiomatic writing, extended techniques, and balance for soloists and chamber ensembles
  • Electronic and Electroacoustic Techniques: Sound design, synthesis, sampling, field recording, introductory live electronics, and stereo or multichannel spatialization
  • Music for Animation, Film, and Games: Spotting sessions, scoring to picture, leitmotif, sync points, and basic adaptive game music (layers and loops)
  • Notation and Presentation: Scores and parts in Dorico or MuseScore, graphic and animated notation, mock-ups, technical riders, mixing, and stems
  • Professional Practice: Revision strategies, working with performers and directors, program notes, calls for scores, and building a portfolio

Course Purpose & Learning Objectives

This course develops each student's ability to carry a single composition through a complete creative cycle, from first sketch to final version. Upon completion, students will be able to:

  • Define a compositional project with a clear artistic intent, medium, duration, and timeline
  • Generate musical materials and develop them into a coherent large-scale form
  • Write idiomatically for their chosen forces: acoustic instruments, electronics, or both
  • Compose music that serves a dramatic or interactive context, such as an animated scene, a film sequence, or a game level
  • Revise a work across several drafts in response to critique, and explain the reasoning behind each revision
  • Prepare professional deliverables: a performance-ready score and parts, a fixed-media file or performance patch, or a mix synchronized to picture with stems
  • Critique their own work and the work of peers using precise technical and aesthetic vocabulary

Course Requirements

Students are expected to:

  • Attend every session and bring the current version of the project (score, audio, session file, patch, or video)
  • Select a project in Week 1 and submit a written project brief in Week 2
  • Submit a new draft at each checkpoint in the schedule, with a short revision log describing what changed and why
  • Complete assigned listening, score study, and reading, and be prepared to discuss it
  • Keep a composition journal of sketches, decisions, and feedback throughout the semester
  • Present the final version of the project in Week 15

Assessment & Grading

Final grades reflect craft, creative growth, and consistent work on the project across the semester. Evaluation is based on the following components:

  • Final Version & Portfolio (35%): The completed work and all deliverables, judged on craft, coherence, and realization of the stated intent
  • Draft Checkpoints & Revision Logs (30%): Four drafts (Weeks 4, 7, 10, and 13), each graded on progress and on how well it responds to earlier feedback
  • Project Brief & Composition Journal (15%): The Week 2 brief and an ongoing record of sketches, decisions, and feedback
  • Listening, Analysis & Participation (20%): Attendance, preparation, analysis assignments, and constructive critique of peer work

Grading Scale: A: 90-100% | B: 80-89% | C: 70-79% | D: 60-69% | F: Below 60%

Attendance Policy

Regular attendance is essential in a project-based studio course, because each session builds on feedback from the one before. Excused absences are not penalized. Each student may have up to two unexcused absences without penalty; each unexcused absence beyond two lowers the final grade by five percentage points. Excessive absences may result in withdrawal from the course. If you must miss class, notify the instructor by email as soon as possible. Excused absences include documented illness, university business, religious observance, and family emergencies.

Course Outline & Schedule

Fall 2026 – Meets Fridays, 12:00 PM – 12:50 PM

Phase I: Concept and Materials (Weeks 1–4)

  • Week 1 (Aug 28): Course Overview; Listening Survey of the Three Tracks (concert, electronic/electroacoustic, and music for animation, film, and games); Project Selection (each student chooses a project, its medium and forces, and a target duration)
  • Week 2 (Sep 4): Project Brief Due (concept, artistic intent, forces or platform, duration, model works, and timeline); for media projects, choose the picture or game scenario and begin a spotting session or cue list
  • Week 3 (Sep 11): Generating Materials: Motive, Pitch Collections, Rhythm, Timbre, and Sound Sources; Sketching and Recording Studies
  • Week 4 (Sep 18): Form Plan and Proportion; Draft 1 Due (materials and form plan, with roughly the first quarter of the piece sketched)

Phase II: Development (Weeks 5–8)

  • Week 5 (Sep 25): Development and Continuity; Analysis of the Model Works Named in Each Brief
  • Week 6 (Oct 2): Track Workshops: Orchestration and Idiomatic Writing (concert); Sound Design and Synthesis (electronic); Scoring to Picture and Sync Points (media)
  • Week 7 (Oct 9): Draft 2 Due (about half of the piece in full texture); Midterm Critique with written feedback
  • Week 8 (Oct 16): Revision Strategies: Responding to Feedback; Tension, Climax, and Pacing

Phase III: Completion (Weeks 9–12)

  • Week 9 (Oct 23): Track Workshops: Extended Instrumental Techniques (concert); Live Electronics or Spatialization (electronic); Adaptive Game Music: Layers, Loops, and Transitions (media)
  • Week 10 (Oct 30): Draft 3 Due (complete draft from beginning to end); Reading Session with Performers or Playback of Mock-Ups
  • Week 11 (Nov 6): Notation and Presentation: Score and Parts in Dorico or MuseScore; Graphic and Animated Notation; Technical Riders
  • Week 12 (Nov 13): Production: Mixing, Mastering, Stems, and Delivery Formats; Final Sync to Picture or Delivery of Game Cues

Phase IV: Final Version (Weeks 13–15)

  • Week 13 (Nov 20): Draft 4 Due (near-final version with score, mix, or synchronized video); Peer Critique
  • Week 14 (Nov 27): Thanksgiving Vacation (no class)
  • Week 15 (Dec 4): Final Version Presentations & Course Wrap-Up; Final Portfolio Due

Class schedule is subject to change as per activities and events that unfold during the semester. Final portfolios are due on the last day of classes. Students are encouraged to submit finished works to calls for scores and concert series, including Electronic Nights at the TTU School of Music.

Final Project Guidelines

Each student completes one original composition, developed from the project selected in Week 1 through four graded drafts to a final version. Suggested scope: 4–6 minutes for concert or electroacoustic works; for media, a complete scene or short of 2–5 minutes, or a set of at least three interactive game cues with transitions. The final portfolio must include:

  • A program note or project statement describing the concept and artistic intent (250–500 words)
  • The final work in the format appropriate to its track:
    • Concert music: full score and parts (PDF), with a recording or a notation-software mock-up
    • Electronic and electroacoustic music: final audio (stereo or multichannel) and, for live electronics, the patch or code with a technical rider
    • Music for animation, film, and games: the final video with synchronized music and stems, or, for games, the looping cues, transitions, and an implementation map
  • All earlier drafts and the revision log showing how the piece changed from version to version
  • Technical documentation of software, hardware, and signal flow (where applicable)

Selected Listening & Reading

Assignments are drawn from the list below and tailored to each project's model works:

  • Concert Music: works by Bartók, Messiaen, Ligeti, Saariaho, and Lachenmann; Samuel Adler, The Study of Orchestration; Elaine Gould, Behind Bars: The Definitive Guide to Music Notation
  • Electronic and Electroacoustic Music: works by Schaeffer, Parmegiani, Truax, Smalley, and Jonty Harrison; Curtis Roads, Composing Electronic Music: A New Aesthetic
  • Music for Animation, Film, and Games: scores by Bernard Herrmann, Joe Hisaishi, Hildur Guðnadóttir, Koji Kondo, and Austin Wintory; Fred Karlin and Rayburn Wright, On the Track; Karen Collins, Game Sound
